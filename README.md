The evolution of Neural Machine Translation (NMT) has been defined by the struggle to overcome the inherent limitations of sequential processing and information bottlenecks. This paper traces the historical trajectory of this evolution, beginning with the early challenges of managing long-range dependencies in sequence-to-sequence models \cite{sutskever2014,cho2014}, and analyzes the transition from fixed-length vector representations to the Attention Mechanism \cite{bahdanau2015,luong2015}, a pivotal innovation that allowed models to dynamically weigh the importance of disparate input tokens. We further examine the impact of the Transformer architecture \cite{vaswani2017} on the current landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs) \cite{brown2020,devlin2019}, and discuss whether the Attention mechanism represents a fundamental convergence between biological cognitive focus and computational intelligence. The review is organized around a chronological--architectural taxonomy and highlights open problems in efficiency, interpretability, and long-range modeling.
